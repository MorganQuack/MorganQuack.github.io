About

I started with visual design. The work kept expanding.

My background began with art, form, composition, and visual expression. Over time, interface, interaction, brand, motion, product logic, design systems, and team leadership stopped feeling like separate disciplines. They became different parts of the same product experience.

I still care about the surface people touch. I also want to know what has to be true behind it for the experience to stay clear, usable, and real.

Chapter 01 / Visual craft

From visual craft to product experience.

Different disciplines accumulated around the same responsibility: make the thing legible, then make it hold.

Chapter 02 / Product experience

Designing the work, not only the screens.

As the scope grew, part of my role became deciding what kind of design work a problem needed: when to stay close to the interface, when to reshape the flow or state model, when brand or motion mattered, when to bring in product and engineering earlier, and how to divide work across UI, UX, brand, and motion without losing the product thread.

The goal was not to make every decision personally. It was to help the team work on the right problem at the right level, then make the result coherent enough to build.

Chapter 03 / Current practice

Still making things.

I use AI-assisted and code-based prototyping to make early product judgments more concrete. For me, Vibe Coding is not a shortcut around design. It is a way to turn a task, state model, permission boundary, error path, or interface hypothesis into something the team can run, inspect, and revise sooner.

Recent work has included AI workspaces, agent governance, security, local tools, reporting, monitoring, and other early product forms. These are prototypes and collaborations at different stages, not replacements for the longer delivery cycles behind the flagship cases.
